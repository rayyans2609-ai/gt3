**Implementation.** `tone=split` selects Day Neutral exposure .35 + Neutral atmosphere endpoints and Night ACES exposure .29 + ACES atmosphere endpoints. The existing continuous theme `nightWeight` drives exposure, atmosphere, reflection, car specular fade and the output mix. Both curves run in the already-warmed output/wash program; no additional pass, render target, texture or late shader switch. Mixing is in linear display RGB, then sRGB is encoded once. This makes intermediate luminance a convex mix of the curve outputs and avoids the darkening caused by blending sRGB code values. Similar endpoint hues limit hue cancellation; measured car C* below checks actual behaviour rather than assuming RGB mixing guarantees saturation. `nightcar=toe` remains strictly Neutral-only and is ignored for split, including its Night endpoint. `carspec=quarter` still fades to normal at Night; all `refl` values work.

**Measurement.** [Full transition samples](transition-metrics.json) record mean D65 L* for road/terrain/sky and mean per-pixel L*/C* within T5's exact depth-tested car silhouette, nearest-upsampled to screenshot size. No thresholded car mask. The nine theme weights are 0, .125, .25, .375, .5, .625, .75, .875, 1; production easing is inverted to obtain deterministic elapsed times. Every point resets the same Day state/pose before advancing. The accepted T1 view contains no visible sky: sky samples come from the real sky mesh rendered through the same output pipeline with world occluders temporarily hidden, at the same camera. This is an isolated sky measurement, not a claimed visible sky region. Normal screenshots supply road, terrain and car measurements. Region fractions and camera transforms are in the JSON.

**Observations.** Neutral preserves Ferrari red better than the calibrated ACES/AgX choices in the T5 diagnosis, but it still looks less vivid than locked's missing-transform output. Quarter specular makes a modest reduction in pale Day sheen and also reduces gloss on Lamborghini/BMW; it is not a large paint-colour correction. At Night, split uses the ACES endpoint and improves dark-body separation compared with locked. Neutral/high + toe lifts car surfaces more directly and leaves the Neutral world curve intact; the tradeoff is brighter car blacks relative to their surroundings. The separate ACES-only column provides the Night endpoint reference. No camera, paint, roughness or metalness was redesigned.

Every measured transition L*/C* sequence is monotonic: no intermediate reversal, brightness dip or chroma rebound. **The largest coarse 0.125-weight changes are not all bounded by locked.** Split's isolated sky reaches 16.89 L* per sample versus locked's 8.34; terrain 4.20 versus 3.67. Lexus car L* reaches 1.55 versus 1.24, and C* 1.12 versus .49. Ferrari car changes are smaller than locked's. These coarse differences must remain visible in the decision: the calibrated Lexus Day→Night range is substantially wider, and the sky's change is concentrated later in blend-weight space. The real easing slows near Night. The [short table](transition-table.md) reports the raw comparison; a claim that every coarse step is smaller than locked would be false. Lexus C* spans 9.07→2.00: even a perfectly uniform eight-interval path requires a largest change ≥0.88, already above locked's 0.49. That absolute coarse cap cannot be met while retaining these requested endpoints.

**Recommendation.** Prefer `tone=split&refl=mid` with the current full Day specular as the Stage 1 starting point, subject to the owner's transition review. It keeps Neutral's stronger Day paint colour and ACES's Night readability without adding a car-specific toe correction. Quarter specular remains available if the owner prefers less Day sheen. Split's tradeoffs are its different sky-fade timing, dark Night blacks, and less vivid Day red than locked. Keep the current defaults until the owner selects a candidate.
